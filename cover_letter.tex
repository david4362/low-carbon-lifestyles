Dear Editor,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript, ``Low-carbon lifestyles deliver broad
emission reductions without automatic rebound,'' for consideration as an Article
in \textit{Nature Climate Change}.

A core assumption in demand-side mitigation modelling is that financial savings
from low-carbon choices are re-spent on other emitting goods, eroding the
climate benefit. This rebound assumption is routinely imposed in
consumption-based mitigation scenarios, yet it has rarely been tested against
comprehensive, population-based consumption data. Using high-resolution
bank-transaction records linked to administrative registers and survey responses
for a random sample of 1{,}311 Swedish single-adult households, we provide one
of the first such tests spanning the entire household consumption bundle.

We find no evidence of automatic rebound: car-free and non-flying households
emit \textit{less}, not more, outside the focal domain despite large financial
savings, with observed indirect emissions falling well below standard
proportional re-spending benchmarks. We further show this favourable pattern
concentrates among households with stronger environmental self-identity ---
implying that the net climate benefit of lifestyle transitions depends on
motivational composition, not avoided direct emissions alone.

These findings speak directly to how rebound is represented in demand-side
mitigation models and to the design of consumption-oriented climate policy ---
an interface of behavioural science, industrial ecology, and mitigation
modelling that we believe will engage the broad, policy-oriented readership of
\textit{Nature Climate Change}.

The work is original and not under consideration elsewhere. We have not had prior discussions with a \textit{Nature Climate Change}
editor about this work. We declare one competing interest, detailed in the
manuscript: D.A.\ founded Svalna inc., which developed the mobile app and
data-collection infrastructure used in the study (and has since ceased
consumer-facing operations); statistical analysis, interpretation of results, and manuscript preparation were conducted independently of Svalna inc.
